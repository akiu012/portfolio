## The Assignment

Build a fully functioning game controller of my own. I started from a CAD file the course provided, and the job was to modify it into a design that was specifically mine, then print it, wire it, and make it work.

## Design

I took my proportions from two places: measurements off an existing physical controller, and measurements of my own hand. Working from the existing controller told me what the baseline geometry needed to be. Working from my hand told me where that baseline did not fit.

![The finished controller: printed case, hand grips, joystick, and button cluster](images/controller_hero.jpg)

The change I care about most is the hand grips. The original file did not have them, and holding a flat shell for an hour is not comfortable. Adding grips that angle down and away from the body gave my hands somewhere to actually sit. In my opinion it made the controller easier and more comfortable to use than the file I started from.

I modeled everything in Onshape and delivered the final .STEP files for each printed component: top case, bottom case, and buttons.

## Fabrication

The case, grips, and buttons were 3D printed. The interior was the harder half of the project: soldering the switch connections, wiring them back to the board, and programming the Arduino that turns those inputs into controller signals.

When I started this project I had very little knowledge of CAD and none of soldering. Soldering is where I struggled the most, especially getting the start and end buttons aligned correctly. Buttons kept failing, and the cause was always the same two things: a weak solder joint or an alignment problem. I redid the soldering several times until every connection was clean and every button worked.

## What I Learned

The obvious skills are CAD modeling and soldering, and I did gain a lot of both. But the part that transferred furthest was troubleshooting: when a button does not respond, working backward through the chain until you find the one joint that is cold.

That, plus patience and attention to detail. I think those matter more for my career than any single tool I learned here.

I genuinely enjoyed this project. It was a lot of fun and a real learning experience, and I appreciate the help the instructors gave me throughout.
